# Competition and Multi-Session Fueling

When you have a competition, a tournament, or a day with multiple training sessions, your fueling strategy shifts from general daily habits to targeted performance support. The goal is to provide accessible energy, maintain hydration, and support recovery between efforts without causing gastrointestinal distress. A successful event-day plan is practical, tested during training, and adaptable to real-life schedules.

## The Foundation: Fueling the Work

Event-day fueling builds on your everyday nutrition. If you enter competition day under-fueled or dehydrated, game-day snacks cannot fully compensate. The priority is ensuring adequate carbohydrate availability to match the demands of your specific event, whether it is a single intense effort or repeated sessions across a long day [1].

Carbohydrates are your primary high-intensity fuel source. As you get closer to the start time, meals and snacks should shift toward easily digestible carbohydrates while reducing fiber, fat, and large amounts of protein, which slow digestion and can cause stomach upset during exertion.

## The Pre-Event Meal: 3 to 4 Hours Before

Your pre-event meal is the final opportunity to top off your energy stores. Aim to eat a substantial meal 3 to 4 hours before your first session. This allows enough time for digestion and absorption.

Focus on familiar, carbohydrate-rich foods with a moderate amount of lean protein. Keep fat and fiber relatively low. 

**Practical Pre-Event Meal Ideas:**
- Oatmeal cooked with milk, topped with a sliced banana and a small drizzle of honey.
- A turkey sandwich on white or sourdough bread with a side of pretzels.
- Rice, grilled chicken breast, and a small portion of cooked carrots (easier to digest than raw vegetables).

## The Top-Off Snack: 30 to 60 Minutes Before

As your event approaches, you may need a small, easily digestible carbohydrate top-off, especially if your pre-event meal was several hours ago or if you are prone to pre-competition nerves that limit your appetite.

This snack should be almost entirely carbohydrates, very low in fiber and fat. 

**Practical Top-Off Snack Ideas:**
- A piece of fresh fruit (like a banana or orange) or applesauce.
- A handful of dry cereal or graham crackers.
- A sports drink or a carbohydrate gel (ensure you drink water with gels).

## Fueling During the Event: Single vs. Multi-Session

Your intra-event fueling depends heavily on the duration and structure of your day.

### Short or Single Sessions (Under 60–90 Minutes)
For events lasting less than 60 to 90 minutes, your pre-event meal and top-off snack are usually sufficient to fuel the work. The primary focus during the event is maintaining hydration with water or an electrolyte beverage, depending on your sweat rate, tolerance, and the environment [2].

### Long or Multi-Session Days (Over 90 Minutes or Repeated Efforts)
When competing in a tournament, a multi-stage race, or a day with multiple workouts, you must actively replenish carbohydrates between efforts to maintain performance and delay fatigue [1].

The time between your sessions dictates what you should eat:

| Time Between Sessions | Fueling Strategy | Practical Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Less than 1 Hour** | Focus on liquid or very easily digested carbohydrates. Keep it light to avoid stomach upset. | Sports drink, carbohydrate gel, fruit snacks, applesauce pouch. |
| **1 to 2 Hours** | Small, carbohydrate-rich snacks with minimal protein and fat. | Banana, granola bar (low fat/fiber), handful of pretzels, white bread with jam. |
| **2 to 4 Hours** | A small, balanced meal emphasizing carbohydrates, with moderate protein. | Half a turkey sandwich, pasta salad with lean chicken, yogurt with fruit. |

*Note: Always practice your between-session fueling during training to understand what your stomach tolerates under stress.*

## Hydration and Electrolytes

Hydration is a continuous process. Begin your event day well-hydrated, indicated by pale yellow urine. 

During the event, drink to thirst and match your fluid intake to your sweat losses, while considering your individual tolerance. Avoid drinking so much that you feel sloshy or gain weight during the event, as excessive fluid intake can lead to hyponatremia (dangerously low blood sodium) [3].

**When to Include Electrolytes:**
Electrolyte replacement, particularly sodium, becomes important in specific scenarios:
- Events lasting longer than 60 to 90 minutes.
- High-intensity efforts in hot or humid environments.
- If you are a "salty sweater" (you notice white residue on your skin or clothes after sweating).
- If you are prone to cramping (though cramping has multiple causes, including fatigue and conditioning).

A sports drink is often the most practical way to consume fluids, carbohydrates, and electrolytes simultaneously during long or repeated sessions.

## Recovery After the Event

Once your final session is complete, the focus shifts to recovery. The goal is to replenish depleted glycogen (carbohydrate) stores, provide protein for muscle repair, and rehydrate [2].

While you do not need to rush to consume a recovery shake the second you finish, aiming to eat a balanced meal within 1 to 2 hours after your event is a practical target. If a full meal is not immediately available or your appetite is low, start with a recovery snack.

**Practical Recovery Strategies:**
- **Immediate Snack (if a meal is delayed):** Chocolate milk, a protein shake with a banana, or a yogurt cup.
- **Recovery Meal:** A balanced plate containing a solid portion of lean protein (e.g., chicken, fish, tofu), a large serving of complex carbohydrates (e.g., sweet potatoes, quinoa, brown rice), and colorful vegetables.
- **Rehydration:** Continue drinking water or electrolyte beverages until your urine returns to a pale yellow color.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Don't try anything new on game day.** Always test your pre-event meals, between-session snacks, and hydration strategies during training.
2. **Match the fuel to the time available.** As the time before or between sessions decreases, shift toward easily digestible carbohydrates and away from fat, fiber, and large amounts of protein.
3. **Hydrate practically.** Start hydrated, drink to thirst or a planned schedule during the event, and consider electrolytes for long, hot, or repeated sessions.
4. **Prioritize the recovery meal.** Replenish carbohydrates and provide protein within 1 to 2 hours after your final event to support muscle repair and prepare for the next day.
