# Fuel Different Fundamentals

The Fuel Different approach is built on a simple premise: nutrition should support your life, not control it. Rather than chasing a perfect diet or following rigid, one-size-fits-all rules, our goal is to help you build sustainable habits that fuel your work, protect your lean tissue, reduce friction, and adapt to the realities of your day. This foundational guide outlines the core principles of the Fuel Different method.

## Individualized Targets Over Universal Rules

Your body, your training, and your life are unique. Because of this, your nutrition targets should be individualized. What works for a professional athlete may not work for a busy parent, and what supports a heavy training phase might be unnecessary during an off-season [1]. 

Fuel Different focuses on establishing targets that match your specific energy demands, body composition goals, and recovery needs. We prioritize total daily energy intake and adequate protein distributed throughout the day, while adjusting carbohydrates and fats based on your activity level and personal preferences [2]. There is no single "best" diet—only the approach that consistently works for you.

## Consistency Over Perfection

A common trap in nutrition is the "all-or-nothing" mindset. If you miss a target or eat something outside your plan, it’s easy to feel like you’ve failed. Fuel Different rejects this perfectionism. 

We emphasize consistency over perfection. Hitting your targets 80% of the time is far more effective than aiming for 100% and giving up when life gets in the way. Consistency builds habits, and habits drive long-term results. When you view nutrition as a series of choices rather than a test you pass or fail, you create a more resilient and sustainable approach to fueling your body.

## Meal Logging as Feedback, Not Judgment

Tracking your food is a powerful tool for understanding your habits, but it is often misused as a source of guilt. In the Fuel Different approach, meal logging is simply a feedback mechanism. 

When you log your meals, you gather data on how much energy you are consuming, how your macronutrients are distributed, and how different foods make you feel. This information allows you and your coach to make informed adjustments. It is not a scorecard of your worth or discipline. Use logging to learn about your patterns, identify areas for improvement, and celebrate your consistency.

## Fuel the Work and Prioritize Recovery

Your training demands energy, and your recovery demands nutrients. Fueling the work means providing your body with the carbohydrates and energy it needs to perform at its best during exercise [3]. Prioritizing recovery means ensuring you consume adequate protein to repair muscle tissue and sufficient overall energy to support adaptation and health [2].

We encourage you to view food as the fuel that makes your training possible and the building blocks that make your recovery effective. This mindset shifts the focus from restriction to empowerment, helping you make choices that support your performance and long-term well-being.

## When to Ask for Individual Support

While this resource library provides foundational education, there are times when general guidance is not enough. You should reach out for individual coaching support if you:

- Feel overwhelmed by trying to hit your targets
- Experience persistent fatigue, poor recovery, or a drop in performance
- Notice signs of under-fueling, such as missed menstrual cycles or frequent illness
- Need help adapting your nutrition to a significant life change, injury, or new training goal
- Want to discuss how to manage nutrition alongside a specific medical condition (note: coaches provide nutritional support, but medical conditions require coordination with a healthcare provider)

Your coach is there to help you translate these principles into a practical plan that fits your life.

---

### Key Takeaways

1. **Individualize your approach:** Focus on targets that match your unique needs and goals.
2. **Aim for consistency:** Hitting your targets most of the time is better than striving for impossible perfection.
3. **Use logging as data:** Treat meal tracking as a tool for learning, not a source of guilt.
4. **Fuel your training:** Provide your body with the energy it needs to perform and recover.
5. **Ask for help:** Reach out to your coach when you need personalized support or adjustments.
